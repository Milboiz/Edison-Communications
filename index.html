How A Bore Runs

The drilling process

A directional bore is a controlled sequence. Here's how a job moves from the first locate to a live line in the ground.

1
// SITE SURVEY

Locate & Plan

Existing utilities are located and marked, and the bore path is designed with the right depth, entry, and exit points.

2
// PILOT BORE

Drill the Pilot Hole

A steerable drill head bores the planned path underground, tracked from the surface to hold line and grade.

3
// REAMING

Back-Ream the Path

The bore is enlarged to the diameter the product line needs, conditioning the hole for a clean pullback.

4
// PULLBACK

Pull the Conduit

Conduit, duct, or pipe is pulled back through the bore in one continuous run — no trench opened along the way.

5
// VERIFY

Test & Prove

The installed line is checked end to end and proofed so it's ready to hand off to the carrier or utility.

6
// RESTORE

Restore the Site

Entry and exit pits are backfilled and graded, and the surface is put back the way it started.

Why Trenchless

Less mess. Less downtime.

Open-cut trenching means closed roads, torn-up yards, and days of repair. Directional drilling installs the same line through a bore no wider than the product — so the surface above stays intact.

For carriers, municipalities, and property owners, that means fewer permits held up, less restoration to pay for, and a job that's in and out.
